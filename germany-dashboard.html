02

Recent trends shaping the German market

What's specifically different about Germany versus the wider European picture.

Consolidation among challengers accelerating

Trade Republic has doubled its customer base to 8M and now offers a full current account (German IBAN, SEPA, 2.25% p.a. on cash); Revolut Business grew ~20% to ~3M German customers. Scale players are converging on full-service banking rather than staying single-product.

Incumbents are re-entering digital SME banking aggressively

ING re-entered German SME banking in September 2024 with a fee-free-for-founders push (up to 12 months, self-employed through GmbH/KG/OHG up to €20M turnover), Deutsche Bank/Postbank now run FYRST, a purpose-built digital-first SME brand, and Commerzbank has repositioned its business account into modular Classic/Premium tiers with a colour-coded cash-flow dashboard — incumbents are fighting fintechs on their own onboarding and UX terms, not just on branch trust.

Embedded lending marketplaces are now table stakes

Qonto launched a financing platform with Banxware giving German SME customers direct access to third-party financing offers inside the account — the same open-marketplace model Tide runs in the UK.

DATEV integration is a German-specific must-have

German accountants and tax advisors standardise on DATEV. A business account without native DATEV export is a hard disqualifier for many SMEs and virtually all Steuerberater-advised businesses.

Privacy sensitivity is structurally higher than UK/US

82% of German consumers are wary of sharing financial data with non-bank providers (Mastercard study) — open banking-based underwriting has to be sold on control and transparency, not just convenience.

Credit conditions have tightened sharply, retail hit hardest

40.5% of SMEs applying for loans in Q2 2026 reported restrictive bank lending practices — a record high, up 6.5pp on the prior quarter — with the retail sector worst-hit: over half of retail mid-market and large firms now report restrictive lending, also a record.

Fintech accounts are the fastest-growing segment for freelancers

Per the 2026 Bundesverband deutscher Banken survey, fintech business accounts are growing faster among sole traders and Freiberufler than any other provider category.

Neobank profitability has arrived, not just growth

N26 posted its first full year of net profitability in 2025 (€501.6M revenue, +13% YoY; €1.6M net income, up from a €42M loss in 2024) and carried the momentum into Q1 2026 (€9.8M net income) — proof the German neobank model can turn a profit, and a signal that financing/credit attach, not just account fees, is where the next profit gains need to come from.

B2B BNPL is a fast-growing adjacent embedded-credit vector

Germany's B2B "buy now, pay later" market is valued at $20.5bn in 2026, led by Billie and Mondu embedding automated credit decisions into marketplaces like Metro and Mercateo. It's a different product than a bank loan, but it proves German SMEs are already comfortable with instant, transaction-data-based credit decisions — a precedent an embedded financing marketplace can build on.

Instant payments are now the mandatory default, not a fintech perk

Under the EU Instant Payments Regulation, every bank and PSP must support real-time SEPA credit transfers 24/7/365 with funds landing in 10 seconds, and — since October 2025 — instant transfers must be priced the same as standard ones. Fast settlement stops being a fintech differentiator in Germany and becomes baseline infrastructure every provider, including incumbents, must offer.

PSD3 moves toward adoption, strengthening open banking's legal footing

The text of PSD3 and a new Payment Services Regulation cleared national-representative approval in April 2026 and is expected to be formally adopted this year, replacing PSD2 with a single framework focused on harmonisation, fraud prevention, and stronger open-banking data rights. A firmer legal footing for data sharing is a direct, gradual counterweight to the 82% German privacy-wariness figure above.

BaFin scrutiny is a live reminder that trust is fragile, even for scaled players

In December 2025 BaFin ordered N26 a second special monitor since 2021, citing "serious deficiencies" in risk and complaint management and the organisation of its lending business, alongside a Dutch mortgage-lending ban and added capital requirements; N26 brought in a new CEO (ex-UBS, effective April 2026) to respond. Even a newly profitable, well-known neobank can be pulled back into visible regulatory enforcement — a reminder that compliance and complaint-handling quality matter as much as growth metrics when selling trust-sensitive products like credit.

Buying mentality & psychology — Germany

German SME buying behaviour has a distinct cultural profile versus the UK/US: higher debt-aversion, higher institutional trust in local banks, and a structurally higher bar for data-sharing trust.

Debt carries more cultural weight in Germany

The German word for debt, Schuld, also means "guilt" — and that linguistic overlap tracks real behaviour: German SMEs are more debt-averse than UK/US peers, prefer retained-earnings financing where possible, and are more sensitive to how a credit offer is framed. The trend line proves it: per KfW's Mittelstandspanel, only 27% of SMEs now consider bank credit for investment financing, down from 66% in 2017 — a structural retreat from borrowing, not just current market caution. Financing pitched as "flexible working capital" lands better than anything resembling a loan hard-sell.

Sparkassen loyalty is a structural trust benchmark

Decades-long relationships with local Sparkassen/ Volksbanken remain the default, especially outside Berlin/Munich. Fintechs aren't competing to replace this relationship outright — the realistic win is becoming the fast, low-friction second account, with financing as the wedge that earns primary-relationship status over time.

KfW-backed financing is seen as legitimate; unknown fintech credit is not (yet)

Because KfW (state development bank) financing is routed through a business's own bank, a loan with the KfW name attached carries automatic institutional trust. A platform that can visibly pass through or reference KfW-backed schemes inherits some of that legitimacy — a materially different psychology from an unbranded "get a loan" fintech pitch.

Privacy is a precondition, not a preference

With 82% of German consumers wary of sharing financial data with non-banks, open-banking-based pre-qualification has to be opt-in, clearly explained, and reversible — presented as convenience under the SME's control, never as a default data-sharing arrangement.

Reading the conditions

  • Restrictive bank lending hit a record high (40.5%) in Q2 2026, up 6.5pp on the prior quarter, even as overall credit demand cooled to its lowest level since Q4 2023 — businesses that do want financing are finding it harder to get from their existing bank, and fewer are even asking.
  • Retail is the hardest-hit sector — over half of retail mid-market and large firms now report restrictive lending, a new record — making retail SMEs a distinct high-need segment for alternative financing.
  • KfW has repeatedly cut its own forecast this year — Q1 2026 corporate credit new business was expected to grow ~3%, but actually contracted 0.3%, and KfW now projects only ~1% growth for Q2 2026, near-stagnant, with a real pickup pushed out to year-end 2026, contingent on the government's fiscal spending package lifting investment demand and easing bank risk perceptions. It has lifted founder funding to €200,000 alongside expanded digitalisation grants in the meantime.
  • The gap between "banks say no" and "KfW wants to lend more" is exactly the space an embedded, open-banking-qualified financing marketplace is built to fill.

Why bundling works commercially in Germany too

Business accounts already generate 66.6% of German neobank revenue — the account is the acquisition surface, and transaction data is the underwriting signal for the higher-margin financing product layered on top, exactly as in the UK market.

Dual-intent: SMEs interested in both an account and financing

The highest-value buyer isn't shopping for an account or a loan — they're evaluating both at once. These are the factors that put an SME into that dual-intent state, and where it converts.

1. Tightening credit pushes account-shoppers to also shop for a lender

With 40.5% of loan applicants facing restrictive bank practices, an SME unhappy with its bank's credit answer is now primed to re-evaluate the whole relationship — including where it banks day-to-day. Dissatisfaction with financing access is becoming a trigger for account switching, not just a separate loan search.

2. Business formation bundles the decision by default

~690,000 people became newly self-employed in Germany in 2025 (KfW Gründungsmonitor 2026), and business registrations were up 10.1% YoY in Q1 2026. A Existenzgründer applying for a KfW-backed founder loan (now up to €200K) needs a business account for the funds to land in before the loan can complete — for new businesses, account and financing aren't sequential decisions, they're effectively one decision made in the same sitting.

3. Open-banking comfort predicts openness to embedded offers

35% of SMB owners already use open banking for cash-flow monitoring, up from 27% a year earlier. That cohort has already crossed the trust threshold needed to accept a pre-qualified, transaction-data-based financing offer from their account provider — the natural first market for embedded credit.

4. Switching is a rare full-relationship review — earn it with transparency

German SMEs switch banks infrequently, so when they do actively compare providers, they evaluate the whole offer, financing included. But with 82% wary of non-bank data sharing, the financing cross-sell has to be opt-in and clearly explained — an assumed or default data-sharing flow will cost more trust than it buys conversion.

Strategic implications for the German market

  1. Make DATEV export and a German IBAN non-negotiable table stakes. These are hard disqualifiers, not nice-to-haves — get them right before investing further in acquisition spend.
  2. Sell financing as "flexible working capital," never as debt. Given Germany's higher cultural debt-aversion, framing and tone in the credit-offer UI matter as much as the underwriting itself.
  3. Lead with KfW-linked or KfW-adjacent financing where possible. Borrowed institutional legitimacy closes the trust gap faster than any amount of fintech-native marketing.
  4. Target the Existenzgründer moment. Account and financing are one decision at formation — integrate with the KfW founder-loan journey so the account isn't a separate step.
  5. Make data-sharing for credit pre-qualification explicit opt-in. With 82% wary of non-bank data use, a transparent, reversible consent flow is a conversion requirement, not just a compliance one.
  6. Position as the fast second account, not a Sparkasse replacement. Win the day-to-day + financing wedge first; primary-relationship status is an earned, longer-term outcome.
