Recent trends shaping the category

Six shifts a challenger selling accounts + credit needs to be building against right now.

Embedded finance becomes the default

Banking, invoicing, payroll, and lending are converging into one app. Standalone "just a current account" offers are commoditised; the account is increasingly the distribution channel for credit, not the product itself.

Neobanks pivot from growth to profitability

After a decade of subsidised free banking, challengers are monetising via paid tiers, FX, and lending margin. Expect fee-tier proliferation and financing products becoming the primary profit line.

AI-led personalisation and underwriting

Open-banking-fed cash-flow data is used for real-time, pre-approved lending offers and AI bookkeeping/expense categorisation — reducing underwriting friction that historically excluded thin-file SMEs.

Regulatory convergence narrows the trust gap

Revolut's UK banking licence (Mar 2026) and rising FSCS-protected e-money structures mean "which licence do you hold" is fading as a differentiator — trust competition shifts to brand and track record.

Open banking scale unlocks better credit decisions

UK open banking passed 12M users (Dec 2024, +50% YoY). Real-time transaction data lets lenders like Tide's marketplace pre-qualify SMEs instantly instead of relying on stale credit-bureau files.

Access-to-finance gap persists for specific groups

Female-led, ethnic-minority-led, and rural/coastal businesses report materially harder access to finance despite strong growth ambition — a targeted-product and trust-building opportunity, not just a risk to manage.

Formation velocity

Companies House registered 900K+ new limited companies in the twelve months to March 2026 — roughly one every 35 seconds. Meanwhile 46% of UK adults report a side hustle in 2026, feeding a steady stream of new sole-trader signups. This is the top-of-funnel Tide-like platforms are built to capture at the point of formation, before an incumbent bank does.

Implication: the sole-trader→limited company transition is a natural upsell moment for financing products, since incorporation is usually paired with a first serious look at credit, payroll and investment.

The solo operator

Sole trader / freelancer
  • Wants: fast setup, low/no fees, simple invoicing, tax-time confidence
  • Credit need: occasional short-term cash-flow smoothing, not growth capital
  • Decision style: fast, self-serve, price- and review-sensitive
Persona B

The scaling limited company

2–50 employees
  • Wants: multi-user access, payroll/expense controls, accountant integration
  • Credit need: working capital, invoice finance, asset finance for growth
  • Decision style: compares 2–3 providers; weighs trust and credit access heavily

The underserved growth business

Female-led, ethnic-minority-led, rural/coastal, thin-file
  • Wants: a lender that will actually say yes, transparent criteria
  • Credit need: growth capital that incumbent banks decline or underprice
  • Decision style: trust- and word-of-mouth driven; loyal once earned

Buying mentality & psychology

Business banking is bought with the head but held onto — or abandoned — with the gut. These are the psychological forces that determine loyalty and willingness to borrow.

Functional parity forces an emotional decision

Once an account can move money reliably, feature checklists converge across providers. At that point trust, transparency and perceived independence from "big-bank" incentives become the deciding factor — not the next feature shipped. Digital capability behaves as a threshold requirement (you're disqualified without it) rather than a differentiator.

"Only 17% named digital tools their most-valued attribute, yet 41% say technology would influence a decision to switch." — table stakes, not the reason to stay.

Fintechs start every relationship with a trust deficit

No branch, no long institutional history, and (for e-money firms) a licence structure most owners don't fully understand. That deficit is closed with transparency, responsiveness, and visible proof of reliability (uptime, FSCS-equivalent safeguarding, reviews) — not with more app features.

Borrowing is emotionally loaded, not a spreadsheet exercise

Small-business finance is personal: fear of rejection, pride in self-sufficiency, and shame around debt all suppress applications even when credit would help the business. Only ~35% of small-business owners rate their own credit as excellent, and debt-management anxiety is rising even as satisfaction improves — credit products need to be framed as low-risk, reversible, and judgement-free to overcome avoidance.

The "yes" is remembered longer than the rate

For historically declined or underpriced segments (female-led, ethnic-minority-led, rural/coastal, thin-file businesses), simply being approved — clearly and respectfully — builds outsized loyalty. Price competitiveness matters less than being taken seriously as a credit risk in the first place.

Where the market is heading

  • Gross SME bank lending: £68bn in 2025, +9% YoY — second-highest in 13 years.
  • 60% of that lending now comes from challenger and specialist lenders, not the traditional big five.
  • 62% of SMEs say they know where to find financing information (+5pp YoY) — awareness is improving faster than actual approval experience.
  • Underserved cohorts (female-led, ethnic-minority-led, rural/coastal) report materially harder access despite strong growth ambition — a clear white-space for a fairer, open-banking-based underwriting model.
  • Invoice finance and open-banking-driven pre-qualified loans (Tide's model: connect the account, get an instant eligibility read) are displacing paperwork-heavy applications.

Why bundling the account with credit works commercially

The current account gives the platform first-party transaction data — the single best underwriting signal for a thin-file small business. That turns the account from a low-margin utility into a distribution engine for higher-margin financing products, which is also why neobanks now draw 67% of revenue from business accounts and are pushing hard into embedded lending rather than competing purely on account fees.

08

Strategic implications for a Tide-like platform

What this research points to for go-to-market and product priorities.

  1. Lead acquisition with speed, land monetisation with credit. Keep account opening near-instant (the 120-second neobank advantage) to win the trust-building "first yes," then use transaction data to make a pre-qualified financing offer within the first weeks of activity — before the SME goes looking elsewhere for a loan.
  2. Sell trust, not features, once parity is reached. Feature checklists no longer differentiate; invest messaging in transparency (safeguarding, FSCS-equivalent protection, plain-English fees) and proof of reliability rather than another app feature launch.
  3. Design credit products to defuse borrowing anxiety. Frame financing as low-commitment and reversible (soft eligibility checks with no credit-score impact, clear declines with next steps) — this directly targets the shame/fear dynamic suppressing applications from creditworthy owners.
  4. Target the incorporation moment. With 900K+ new limited companies formed a year, integrate with company-formation flows to capture SMEs before their first "real" bank relationship forms.
  5. Build explicitly for underserved segments. Female-led, ethnic-minority-led and rural/coastal SMEs are underserved by incumbents and disproportionately loyal once approved fairly — a defensible, high-LTV niche competitors are not fully contesting.
  6. Treat the account as underwriting infrastructure, not the product. The commercial logic across the category (67% of neobank revenue from business accounts, rising embedded-finance adoption) confirms the account's job is to generate the cash-flow data that makes the credit decision — price and design it accordingly.
